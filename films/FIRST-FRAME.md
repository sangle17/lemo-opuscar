# First frame: rules for the first 3 seconds of a Short (and the first 30 s of a film)

Written 2026-10-27, after the channel's own analytics: on every Short, "Viewed vs. swiped away" is only 30–50%, against a target of 60–70%+. That is the biggest single loss. This file works alongside CHANNEL-RULES §2 and §11 and the two STYLE.md files. It never asks a style to stop being itself.

**Evidence levels:** **Strong** = platform definition or replicated lab/field research. **Moderate** = large observational data or a credible single test. **Practice** = what successful channels visibly do (studied below). **Own** = this channel's analytics. Vendor blog percentages ("+25% retention with captions") are left out: none of them gives a method.

---

## 1. What the evidence says

**The platform.** In the Shorts feed the viewer never sees a thumbnail. The first moving frame, the first words and the sound *are* the thumbnail. YouTube Studio's "Viewed vs. swiped away" (Content → Shorts) is the share of feed impressions where the viewer stayed instead of swiping. YouTube publishes no threshold. It says to compare against your own Shorts (YouTube Help, *Content tab analytics tips – Shorts*). **Strong** (definition).

**Large observational data.** Paddy Galloway and Chris Gileta analysed about 5,400 Shorts from 33 channels (2023). The best performers sat at roughly 70–90% viewed. Below about 60%, distribution fell away. Long Shorts paid off only when view duration stayed high. **Moderate.** These figures are known only from secondary write-ups and the original thread.

**Attention research.** **Strong**, from lab work. Nothing here is Shorts-specific, but it is what governs the first half-second:
- The *onset* of motion, and objects that loom or expand, capture attention automatically. A static or slowly moving display does not (Abrams & Christ 2003; Franconeri & Simons 2003).
- Faces draw the first fixations even when the viewer is doing a task. Contrast and saliency draw them in free viewing (Cerf, Harel, Einhäuser & Koch 2008; Itti & Koch 2001).
- In ads, "feature complexity" hurts: clutter, many small elements, low figure–ground contrast. "Design complexity" helps: one clear, rich object (Pieters, Wedel & Batra 2010, *J. Marketing*).
- Viewers zap TV and online ads less when a face shows joy or surprise early. A brand or a slow build-up at the start raises zapping (Teixeira, Wedel & Pieters 2010 and 2012, *Marketing Science* / *JMR*). **Moderate to strong.** Online video ads are the closest match to the swipe decision that this research covers.
- Eye-tracking of TikTok viewers found a roughly 3-second engagement threshold, with text often fixated before faces. **Weak:** an MSc thesis with n = 20 (Twente 2025) and an exploratory study with n = 16 (*Communication Today* 2026).

**Captions.**
- Captions improve comprehension, attention and memory of video, for hearing viewers too. More than 100 studies agree (Gernsbacher 2015, *Policy Insights from the Behavioral and Brain Sciences*). **Strong.**
- In a Verizon Media/Publicis survey of 5,616 US adults (2019), 69% said they watch with the sound off in public and 25% even in private. 80% said they are more likely to finish a video that has captions. **Moderate:** this is what people say, not what they do.
- Facebook's own test found +12% view time for captioned feed ads. **Moderate,** reported secondhand.
- YouTube Shorts plays with sound on by default, so the sound-off case is weaker there than on Facebook. An `.srt` file shows only to viewers who switch CC on. **Judgment.**

**Own data.**
- The one Short that held viewers through the middle had stakes, an ending that echoed the hook, and a burned-in caption strip ("By 1914, only two African countries…").
- Quiz outros ("Did you guess it? Tell me below") lost viewers sharply.
- Earlier dense opening frames got fewer than 20 views each (CHANNEL-RULES, evidence section).

### What successful faceless history and geography Shorts do in 0–3 s (Practice)

I took the opening 0, 1 and 2.5 s frames of 10 top Shorts (study only, clips deleted):

| RealLifeLore · "B2 bombed Afghanistan from Missouri" (5.3 M) | A dark globe already panning, a red dot, a stamped "DIEGO GARCIA" label. Captions from word 1. The camera keeps moving. |

| Kurzgesagt · "The Moon's invisible threat" (5.3 M) | A big two-colour headline ("Silent Killer On The Moon!") over a dark figure with glowing red eyes, a face-like threat. Small captions. A hard cut to a rocket at about 2 s. |
| Zack D. Films · "How electrical outlets work" (60 M) | A macro close-up of a hand pushing a plug, mid-action at frame 0. The camera keeps pushing *through* the wall and at 2.5 s reveals a tiny man inside. One continuous push, and the surprise lands by 2.5 s. |
| The Armchair Historian · "Why did soldiers fight in lines?" (2.8 M) | A soldier's face fills the frame. The line moves. Bold caps captions in the centre band from 1 s. |
| Simple History · "Maus tank: 207-ton monster" (0.84 M) | A brand bar and a persistent headline box hold the hook in text. The tank is in the scene. A cut at about 2 s. |
| The Infographics Show · "This country has committed the most war crimes" (4.5 M) | A full frame of burning buildings, high red and orange contrast. A three-line headline pops in by 1 s. |
| Geography Now · "The 'new' UN map" (1.1 M) | Not faceless (a presenter's face plus a collage). Captions from word 1. |

What they all share:
1. The subject fills the frame from frame 0. Nobody opens on an establishing shot or an empty tool.
2. Something moves at frame 0.
3. The colour is saturated and high in contrast: no pale-on-pale.
4. A face or human figure appears whenever the story has one.
5. There is a picture change by about 2 s.
6. The hook is readable as text: burned captions or a persistent headline.
7. The voice starts on frame 0.

### This channel's current frames 1 (extracted at 0 / 1 / 2.5 s)

They share five faults:
1. A pale ground (light-table white or cream paper) with pale content (pencil, light hatching, grey satellite).
2. The loupe ring or roundel ring is the strongest shape on screen, and what it frames is still empty or blurred.
3. The subject the first sentence names ("this man", "this field", "this island", "this base") is small, unreadable or not there yet.
4. Almost nothing changes between 0 and 2.5 s.
5. No human face fills the frame, even where one exists (Freud and Stalin in Vienna, Ötzi, Silvia Morella de Palma).

## 2. Ranked rules (at most 12)

| # | Rule | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frame 1 shows the subject the first sentence names, filling the frame:** at least 50% of the frame height, centred between 20% and 70% of the height. "This man / this island / this field" must *be* on screen at the word "this". | The swipe decision is made on this frame. Deictic words pointing at an empty frame break the promise. | Strong (platform, saliency) · Practice (all 10) · Own |
| 2 | **Already in motion at frame 0.** Start the move mid-curve (a push, develop front, pull, line being drawn) so a visible change happens before 0.5 s. Never ease in from rest, never fade in, never start on black. | Motion *onset* and looming capture attention. A still frame doesn't. | Strong (lab) · Practice |
| 3 | **High figure–ground contrast at frame 0.** The darkest darks and the one signal or flat-ink colour sit *on the subject*. Pale pencil on a pale table, or grey on grey, never opens a Short. | Saliency drives the first fixation. Clutter and low contrast ("feature complexity") hurt. | Strong (lab, ad research) |
| 4 | **A face or human figure first whenever the story has one.** A real archive portrait in Map Room. An engraved figure in a roundel, cut close, in Atlas Engraving. | Faces win the first fixation. Early faces reduce zapping. | Strong / Moderate |
| 5 | **One object, not a scene.** At most one tag or label on screen in 0–3 s. No whole table, no whole plate, no four small portraits. | Clutter hurts. Dense openings scored lowest here. | Moderate · Own |
| 6 | **A state change at 1.2–2.0 s that adds information.** A style-native "cut": the loupe lifts and drops on the next object, a develop front, an ink pull, a burnish, a log-zoom pull-out. Then a second one by about 3 s. | Each beat of the voice gets a new picture. Every top Short changes at about 2 s. | Practice |
| 7 | **The picture answers the voice in the same 0.3 s.** At the number, the number appears (a reading card or engraved figure). At the name, the face appears. | Coherence: matching picture and narration helps people take in a fact. | Moderate (multimedia learning) |
| 8 | **Voice from frame 0, at full energy, with concrete first words** (a date, a number, a name, "this + noun"). No breath or room tone before it. The 0.8 s post-hook pause still keeps the picture moving. | Already channel rule §2. Enthusiasm holds people. | Strong (Guo 2014) · Own |
| 9 | **The hook line is legible as text in 0–3 s** (see the caption recommendation in §7), set in the style's own lettering. | Sound-off viewers and fast deciders read it. The one held Short had it. | Moderate · Own |
| 10 | **Never open on the tool or the stage:** an empty loupe, an empty roundel ring, bare paper, the table switching on, a title, a wide establishing shot, a logo. | These are build-up, and build-up raises zapping. | Moderate (ad research) · Own |
| 11 | **Keep 0–3 s readable through the UI:** subject and text above the bottom 15% and below the top 8%, at least 60 px from the sides. | The Shorts UI covers those areas. | Platform |
| 12 | **Close the loop, don't ask.** The last line echoes the hook ("Up from just two"). No "Did you guess it? Tell me below". A clean loop into frame 1 turns the end into a replay. | Quiz outros dropped sharply. Replays count as views (since 2025-03-31). | Own · Platform |

Measure: re-cut one Short at a time, change only 0–3 s, and compare its "viewed" share with the channel's other Shorts from the same week.

## 3. Map Room first frame (STYLE.md stays intact)

The Map Room's own vocabulary is enough. The loupe is the door, objects are real, one signal colour, transitions by land, push, rise and lift. The trick is to **start inside the move, not before it.** Using Variation space "a loupe already on a plate", and doing it on the *subject*, is allowed by §11 ("Three openings").

**Pattern A: Already in the lens (default).** Frame 0 is the moment *after* a loupe landing: the lens interior covers 85–110% of the frame width, so the ring is mostly outside the frame or is a thin arc at the edges. The object inside is the subject at reading zoom: a portrait plate, a satellite crop with the craft centred, a document line. The push is already moving (dolly or develop front at mid-speed). At 0.3–0.6 s the signal mark lands on the subject: a ring, an underline, or a reading card with the number. At 1.2–2.0 s the loupe rises and drops straight onto the second object, in the style's 0.4 s ease-out. That is the cut. *Never:* the full ring with a blurred or empty interior.

**Pattern B: Into the photograph first.** For a story with a place or person, open in *photograph space*: the real photo fills the frame, a subject layer is drifting, parallax is already running. The table tag shows at the top edge (number in signal) so the object still reads as archive. At about 1.5 s, rise out of the lens to the table and the next object (or drop onto a second dated plate). This is the Map Room's most cinematic move. Spend it in second 0, not second 20.

**Pattern C: The number off the page.** For number hooks, frame 0 is a tight push on the line of type that holds the figure. The line is already lit and the rest dimmed. The figure lifts out into a dark reading card (74–120 px mono) *with the voice*. At 1.5 s, two records side by side (native move) or the loupe moves to the object the number is about.

**Lettering for a burned hook line:** the STYLE §4 caption strip (dark plate, 3 px signal edge, sans), raised to sit at about 64–74% of the height so it clears the Shorts UI.

**Never open a Map Room Short on:**
- the whole table;
- the light table switching on;
- an empty loupe gliding or landing on an empty area;
- a pencil-on-animation-paper reconstruction (pale on pale; use it after 3 s);
- a full-frame handwriting page with no single readable word;
- a blurred satellite crop.

## 4. Atlas Engraving first frame (STYLE.md stays intact)

STYLE §5 already says "Frame 1 is never blank: start the first figure a few seconds into its cutting". In practice, "a few seconds" left 70% bare paper. Move the start point to **when the subject first reads**: outline done, first hatching laid, one ink pulled. Inks are the style's contrast weapon, so a pull should be underway at frame 0.

**Pattern A: Roundel at full width, ink mid-pull.** Frame 0: a roundel at about 2.5× fills the width, its ring complete. The figure or place inside is already outlined and half hatched, and a flat ink (vermilion, ochre, teal) is crossing it behind the squeegee front, so colour arrives at 0.2–0.8 s on the first stressed word. The plate lettering for the subject (INDIA, FRANCE) is being cut glyph by glyph and is legible by 1 s. At about 1.5–2 s, a log-zoom pull-out shows where it sits (the native "where that thing sits" move). That is the cut.

**Pattern B: A state change on the hook word.** For "changes country" or "moved the border" hooks, open on the *finished* state of one side. At the verb (0.8–1.5 s), the second ink is pulled from the opposite direction, or the roundel is burnished out right to left and re-cut. The surprise *is* the state change, so it must land in second 1, not second 3.

**Pattern C: A close figure, then the plate.** For a person (Ötzi, a surveyor), frame 0 is the figure's head and shoulders cut close, heavy outline, cross-hatched shadow, one ink on the object they hold. At 1.5 s, pull out to the roundel and the leader to the map.

**Lettering for a burned hook line:** STYLE §4 allows "script on a deckled paper slip above the bottom 15%". For legibility at Shorts speed, use the plate's own *italic* (Bodoni Moda italic, at least 44 px) on that slip. Keep the script (Pinyon) for dates only. This is a small deviation from STYLE §4, so ask the user. Text is cut or written, never faded.

**Never open an Atlas Engraving Short on:**
- an empty or half-scribed roundel ring;
- bare cream paper over more than 40% of the frame;
- the bare neatline;
- a figure that is still only outline;
- the whole plate at 1×;
- a mannequin-like figure (STYLE §9).

## 5. Re-cut checklist: change only the picture in 0–3 s (the voice stays)

1. Read the first sentence in the `.srt` and mark its nouns, number and deictic words ("this …"). Mark the time each one is spoken.
2. Extract frames at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 s and tile them at 240 px wide (the feed decision happens at about phone-thumb size).
3. At frame 0: can you name the subject in under 0.5 s at 240 px? Does it fill at least 50% of the height? If not, pick the strongest real object or figure for the first noun and start there (rule 1).
4. Is there a visible change between frames 0 and 0.5? If not, shift the move's start so frame 0 sits mid-curve (`t0 −= 1.5 s` on the opening camera keys is often enough), or start an ink pull or develop front (rule 2).
5. Is the subject the darkest/most saturated thing on screen, and is the signal or ink colour on it? If not, choose a different object, push in, or pull the ink earlier (rule 3).
6. Is there a face in the story? Put it first (rule 4).
7. Count tags and labels in 0–3 s: at most 1 (rule 5).
8. Is there a style-native state change between 1.2 and 2.0 s, and another by about 3 s? (rule 6)
9. Does each named thing or number appear within 0.3 s of being spoken? (rule 7)
10. Is the hook line on screen in the style's lettering, if the caption rule is adopted? (rule 9)
11. Check that nothing important sits in the bottom 15% or the top 8%, and that text is at least 60 px from the edges (CHANNEL-RULES §11.10).
12. Blend at about 3 s into the *existing* picture. A loupe lift or rise (Map Room) or a pull-out or burnish (Atlas) hides the seam. Keep the soundtrack sample-identical, and re-time foley only for the new opening moves (loupe clack, pull).
13. Run the style test (§11.5) on the new frame 0. It must look like the demo, not like a poster.
14. Log the before and after "viewed" share in the film's `review/`.

## 7. Caption recommendation

**Burn in a style-native hook and caption strip on Shorts, and keep delivering the `.srt`.**

This needs a change to CHANNEL-RULES §2 and §5, which today allow on-screen text "only where the style draws it". That is the user's decision. Both STYLE.md files already define a burned-caption form, so the look stays inside the style.

Why:
- The one Short that held viewers had a strip.
- Captions help comprehension and memory (strong evidence).
- Sound-off viewing is common on Facebook and in public (moderate evidence).
- Every top channel studied puts the hook on screen as text.
- Without CC switched on, the `.srt` doesn't help a feed viewer.

How:
- **Map Room:** the STYLE §4 strip: dark plate rgba(14,15,17,0.74), 3 px signal edge, the film's sans at 40–48 px, white. Raised to 64–74% of the frame height, not the very bottom.
- **Atlas Engraving:** a deckled paper slip with the plate italic in ink (see §4), above the bottom 15%.
- **Chunks** of 3–6 words in sentence phrases, timed to the voice. No word-by-word pop and no colour-highlighted karaoke: those are guru styles, not this channel's.
- **Hold** each chunk at least max(1.2 s, speech + 0.3 s). If a chunk is shorter, merge it with the next one.
- **From frame 0** the first chunk is on screen with the first word.
- **Placement:** the strip never covers the subject. Place the subject in the upper two-thirds.
- **Optional:** keep the strip only for 0–10 s (the hook and the setup), then hand over to the style's own labels. This is the minimum version to test if a full strip feels too heavy.
- **Test:** alternate 4 Shorts with the strip and 4 without, in the same weeks, and compare "viewed" share and the retention at 50%.

## 8. Long-video openings (first 30 s)

- **0–5 s:** the most striking image of the film, the one the title and thumbnail promise, with the voice from frame 0. No logo, no title card, no "in this video". An early mismatch with the title causes the early drop (CHANNEL-RULES evidence: strong).
- **5–15 s:** the stakes or the question in one sentence, with the promise that it will be answered ("and the answer is stranger than the map").
- **15–30 s:** the first real piece of evidence, plus a preview of the payoff image without giving the answer.
- The same rules 1–7 hold for the first frame, at 16:9. Picture changes every 2–4 s (practice). The first chapter marker comes only after the hook.
- In Map Room, the "table switching on" or "whole table" opening may come *after* the cold open, at about 15–30 s, as the context beat. In Atlas Engraving, "the finished plate" or the bare neatline fits there too.
- Re-hook at about 20–30 s with a turn ("But here's the problem…"). Practice.
